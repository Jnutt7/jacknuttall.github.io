Effects of Asymmetries in Merging Dual Jet Streams on Downstream Velocity Profiles and Turbulent Flow Characteristics

Experimenter — with Jack Clark & Fion Govad

A fluid dynamics lab study on how introducing an asymmetric incidence angle between two parallel free jets changes where their flows merge and combine downstream, and how quickly turbulence develops. Using a pitot-tube traverse on a gantry carriage system, our team mapped velocity and turbulence profiles for both a symmetric parallel-jet baseline and an angled configuration, then compared where each configuration's merge point and combined point landed relative to jet diameter (D).

  • Built and instrumented a two-jet rig — two parallel round free jets of diameter D set a distance S apart — with pitot-tube velocity measurements taken across both x and y via a gantry carriage system.
  • Tested a fully symmetric parallel configuration against an asymmetric configuration with one jet tilted at an incident angle θ (converging), up to 10°.
  • Found that introducing asymmetry pulled both the merge point and combined point much closer to the jet exits: merge point dropped from ~5D (parallel) to ~1–3D (angled), and combined point from >14D to ~7–10D.
  • Measured centerline turbulence intensity at increasing downstream distances (1D–14D) for both configurations, finding turbulence developed sooner and peaked higher (~50%) in the asymmetric case.
  • Co-authored with Jack Clark and Fion Govad for AME 341B, Spring 2025.
